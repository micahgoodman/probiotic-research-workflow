# Probiotic Evidence Catalogue

17 claims across 1 researched entity of 97 on the roster. Built 2026-08-09.

Generated by `tools/build.py` from `strains/`. Identity is joined from `roster.json`, outcome labels and body systems from `vocabulary/outcomes.json`. Do not hand-edit.

Tiers: **A** high (SR/meta-analysis, bias appraised) · **B** moderate (RCTs, no SR) · **C** low · **D** very low (case reports) · **P** preclinical (animal or in vitro)

Human and preclinical evidence are separate ladders. Where an outcome carries both, they appear as separate lines under one heading: preclinical work never raises a human tier, and a human finding never suppresses the animal row.

What this is, and is not: an evidence map graded from abstracts and published systematic reviews. Full-text critical appraisal is delegated to the review literature, which is why tier A requires one. Doses and effect sizes are as the sources reported them, not verified against full texts. Abstracts overstate positive results and the catalogue inherits that; recorded nulls and the tier ladder are the counterweight, not a cure. A study that never confirms its exact strain is excluded, so the table errs toward missing evidence rather than misattributing it — and an absent row means no pass found or searched the outcome, never that the effect is disproven.

See `coverage.md` for what has not been researched yet.

## Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG (ATCC 53103)

`lacticaseibacillus-rhamnosus-gg` · single_strain · live · triaged deep  
Also known as: Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG, LGG, Culturelle, Enflora LGG  
Reviewed 2026-08-07 (oldest of 6 passes)  
**Incomplete:** gi, immune, metabolic, mental-health, mechanistic, safety still owe targeted searches.  

> **GI.** LGG's best-supported GI outcome is antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention in children (tier A); acute gastroenteritis treatment, infant colic, and IBS abdominal pain show moderate benefit (tier B/C), several capped by heterogeneity or a fragile subgroup effect rather than by absence of evidence. A notable caution: RCT-pooled evidence favors LGG for necrotizing enterocolitis prevention in preterm infants, but a real-world NICU cohort found the opposite direction and rare sepsis cases exist in that population - recorded here and flagged for the safety pass. Traveler's diarrhea prevention was tested and came back essentially null.

> **Immune.** LGG's clearest Immune benefit is reduced upper respiratory tract infection incidence in children (tier B), alongside a related otitis media finding first raised here as a proposal and since reconciled into the vocabulary as imm-otitis-media-incidence (leads in gaps). Allergic rhinitis and ventilator-associated pneumonia both show early positive signal overturned or unreplicated by larger, more rigorous trials, and are recorded here as null/conflicting rather than benefits - the PROSPECT VAP trial in particular is a large definitive RCT, not a thin literature. A cow's-milk-allergy oral-tolerance finding turned up with no matching outcome id and was proposed rather than force-fit; it is now imm-cma-oral-tolerance (leads in gaps).

> **Metabolic.** This pass found only thin, mixed-quality Metabolic evidence for LGG. The one usable finding - stabilized HbA1c versus a placebo rise, in non-diabetic older adults - does not extend to existing type 2 diabetes, where a separate small trial found no fasting-glucose benefit. Lipid findings that surfaced were not gradeable for LGG specifically: one used a corn-fiber synbiotic that confounds attribution to the strain, and another used an unidentified L. rhamnosus product from a different manufacturer with no strain designation given, so it cannot be confirmed as GG. No strain-confirmed evidence was found for body weight, blood pressure, visceral fat, or NAFLD; the one gestational-diabetes trial in this literature used HN001, a different L. rhamnosus strain, not GG.

> **Mental health.** GG-specific Mental health evidence is thin and easy to over-claim: most of the well-known 'probiotic psychobiology' literature under the rhamnosus name (JB-1, HN001, CNCM I-3690) is a different strain and is excluded here. What remains for GG itself: a human cognition benefit confined to older adults with pre-existing cognitive impairment, and consistent but small (2-study) anxiolytic evidence in stressed rodents. Depression, general stress/cortisol, sleep, and autism-associated symptoms turned up no gradeable GG-specific human evidence in this pass.

> **Mechanistic.** GG's mechanistic literature is large but strain-attribution is the recurring hazard: several of the most cited gut-brain-axis mechanisms (GABA receptor modulation, vagal-nerve dependence) belong to strain JB-1, not GG, and are excluded here. What is strain-confirmed for GG itself: transient human colonic colonization documented by direct biopsy recovery, in vitro competitive exclusion of Salmonella and E. coli via steric hindrance, tight-junction protein upregulation in animal barrier-injury models, and human RCT evidence that GG supplementation reliably alters its own detectable abundance without improving overall gut microbiome diversity.

> **Safety.** GG is one of the most consumed probiotic strains in the world and is safe for the general healthy population, but a real and strain-confirmed bacteremia signal exists in specific vulnerable groups: patients with central venous catheters or other indwelling lines, immunocompromised/immunosuppressed patients (including active inflammatory bowel disease on corticosteroids), infants with short bowel syndrome, and preterm/NICU infants. All confirmed cases trace to gut mucosal translocation in a host with a compromised barrier, not to healthy oral consumption. A large ICU RCT (PROSPECT, already noted in immune.json) also found L. rhamnosus recovered from a normally sterile site at a low but significantly elevated rate versus placebo, consistent with this same risk pattern in critically ill adults.

#### reduces IBS abdominal pain

`gi-ibs-pain`

- **humans** · benefit · tier **B** — mostly children with IBS (4 of 5 RCTs pediatric; diagnostic criteria varied by trial)
  - significant reduction in abdominal pain severity, I2=25.9% (SR of 5 LGG-only RCTs; pooled RR/MD not reported in abstract) · 5 studies · 318 participants · mixed · independent replication: yes
  - 6x10^9 CFU/day (effect not replicated at 2x10^10 or 4x10^10 CFU/day in the same pool) · 8 weeks (effect not seen at 4 or 6 weeks) · capsule
  - human_meta · bias: sr_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Maslennikov et al. 2026 strain-specific SR (single-strain LGG-vs-placebo RCTs only), RoB2-assessed. Pain was the only outcome poolable for LGG - 'other IBS symptoms and quality of life indicators could not be meta-analyzed'. The pooled benefit is fragile: it was lost across all subgroups when trials were stratified by IBS diagnostic criteria, and the dose-response was non-monotonic (benefit only at ~6x10^9 CFU/day, not at 2x10^10 or 4x10^10). Modest pooled N and that inconsistency cap this at B rather than A.*
  - roster_source's shorthand cites this SR under gi-ibs-global; the outcome it actually grades is abdominal pain, so the claim is filed under gi-ibs-pain. Other IBS symptoms and QoL were not poolable for this strain.
  - Sources: PMID:41682832

#### prevents necrotizing enterocolitis in preterm infants

`gi-nec-prevention`

- **humans** · benefit · tier **B** — preterm / very-low-birth-weight infants
  - RR 0.50 [0.26, 0.93], p=0.03, NEC >=Stage II (SR of 5 LGG-only RCTs) · 5 studies · 851 participants · mixed · independent replication: yes
  - 1x10^9-6x10^9 CFU/day across trials · drops
  - human_meta · bias: sr_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Ananthan et al. 2024 strain-specific SR (Cochrane methodology), RCT-only pooled estimate for LGG as sole probiotic. The RR is real but close to the null (CI upper bound 0.93) on only 5 trials. Consistency is marked mixed because a separate single-center before/after cohort (outside this SR) found NEC incidence rose after routine LGG adoption (10.2% to 16.8%), and rare LGG sepsis has been reported in preterm infants specifically - a genuine real-world caution alongside the RCT-level benefit. That tension, not the RCT evidence alone, is why this caps at B rather than A.*
  - **Safety:** A conflicting single-center observational cohort found higher NEC incidence after routine LGG use in a NICU; rare LGG sepsis has been reported in preterm infants. See safety-bacteremia in safety.json.

#### prevents ventilator-associated pneumonia

`imm-vap`

- **humans** · no effect · tier **C** — critically ill adults on mechanical ventilation (ICU)
  - PROSPECT (n=2653): HR 1.03 [0.87-1.22], P=.73, no difference in VAP; earlier Morrow et al. 2010 pilot (n=146) found benefit (19.1% vs 40.0% VAP, P=.007), not replicated at scale · 2 studies · 2,799 participants · conflicting · independent replication: yes
  - 1x10^10 CFU twice daily (PROSPECT, enteral); Morrow 2010 dose not stated in abstract · throughout mechanical ventilation (PROSPECT)
  - human_rct · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: none
  - *PROSPECT (Johnstone et al. 2021, JAMA), a large multicentre RCT across 44 ICUs, found no VAP benefit and no difference in mortality, ICU length of stay, C. difficile, or antibiotic use, superseding an earlier small single-center pilot (Morrow et al. 2010) that had found a large benefit. No SR pools the two; direction taken from the larger, more recent, better-powered trial per source policy, with the conflict recorded as 'conflicting' rather than silently dropping the earlier finding. This is not a thin literature - PROSPECT alone is adequately powered - but the disagreement between trials and lack of formal bias appraisal caps it at C.*
  - **Safety:** PROSPECT found L. rhamnosus recovered from a normally sterile site in 15/1318 (1.1%) probiotic-group patients vs 1/1332 (0.1%) controls. See safety-bacteremia in safety.json.
  - Sources: PMID:34546300; PMID:20522788

### Mechanistic

#### improves gut barrier integrity

`mech-gut-barrier`

- **animals** · benefit · tier **P** — newborn Wistar rats, gliadin-induced enteropathy model; C57BL/6J mice, DSS-induced colitis model
  - co-administration with gliadin challenge restored villus length (+53.5% vs gliadin-alone, P<0.05) and upregulated tight-junction/adhesion genes (ZO-1 18.1-fold, occludin 10.9-fold, beta-catenin 6.9-fold, E-cadherin 8.3-fold by qPCR; beta-catenin +590.6% and E-cadherin +39.9% by Western blot, both P<0.05) versus gliadin alone (Orlando et al.); separately, GG restored occludin expression and reduced fecal/serum IL-6 (P=0.049 / P=0.040) in a DSS-colitis mouse model via upregulated arginine metabolism (Lin et al. 2025) · 2 studies · consistent · independent replication: yes
  - 1x10^9 CFU/day x10 days (Orlando); 10^8 CFU single gavage (Lin) · 10 days (Orlando); 11-day DSS challenge + 5-day recovery (Lin) · gavage
  - animal · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Preclinical, tier P by definition. Both studies confirmed strain GG (ATCC 53103) and used a barrier-injury challenge model (gliadin enteropathy; DSS colitis) rather than healthy-animal baseline, so the finding is specifically about barrier restoration under insult, not baseline barrier enhancement. A frequently cited Journal of Dairy Science tight-junction study (ZO-1/MLCK upregulation) was excluded after verification showed it used a different Lactobacillus rhamnosus strain, OLL2838, not GG - see gaps.*
  - Sources: PMID:30405050; PMID:40418622

#### shifts gut microbiome composition

`mech-microbiome-composition`

- **humans** · no effect · tier **B** — hospitalized adult VRE carriers on antibiotics; formula-fed infants with colic
  - no significant alpha-diversity difference vs placebo in hospitalized adults (Krishna et al., n=10 LGG/11 placebo microbiome subset, richness p=0.148-0.713, Shannon p=0.078-0.813; beta-diversity difference P=0.006 attributed to baseline imbalance, not treatment); in infants, alpha diversity was significantly LOWER in the LGG arm than control (P=0.022) and beta diversity differed at study end (P=0.05), though LGG's own relative abundance was higher in that arm (P<0.001) (Cohen-Silver et al., n=35/36) · 2 studies · 57 participants · mixed · independent replication: yes
  - 60x10^9 CFU/day (adults, 4 weeks); dose in infant formula not stated in abstract (3 weeks) · 3-4 weeks across the two trials · capsule (adults); infant formula (infants)
  - human_rct · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Two independent RCTs, both strain-confirmed GG, in very different populations, without a pooling SR - B per the multiple-independent-RCT rule. Consistency marked mixed rather than conflicting: neither trial found LGG improves overall microbiome diversity, but the direction in infants (significantly lower alpha diversity on LGG) is a genuine negative signal beyond simple nulls, worth surfacing rather than averaging away. GG's own detectable abundance rising in both settings shows the strain is present and active without translating into a diversity benefit.*
  - The adult trial's primary endpoint (VRE eradication) was also null - 57% LGG vs 56% placebo cleared at 4 weeks - consistent with the absence of a broader microbiome effect.
  - Sources: PMID:35475684; DOI:10.1186/s12887-022-03647-x

#### inhibits pathogen growth or adhesion

`mech-pathogen-inhibition`

- **in_vitro** · benefit · tier **P** — human Caco-2 intestinal epithelial cell monolayers
  - Salmonella Typhimurium adhesion reduced from ~16.4% to ~9.5% under thermal-stress conditions (P=0.001) and cytotoxicity reduced from 30.5% to ~7.3% (P=0.001), Caco-2 model; separate studies show competitive exclusion/displacement of E. coli and Salmonella spp. from Caco-2 cells and intestinal mucus via steric hindrance, strain-dependent in degree · 2 studies · consistent · independent replication: yes
  - in_vitro · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Preclinical/in vitro row, tier P by definition. Confirmed strain GG (ATCC 53103) in both the quantified Salmonella thermal-stress study (Caco-2, PMID:19542213-family literature) and the broader Collado-group competitive-exclusion/mucus-adhesion literature. GG showed no protective effect on unstressed Caco-2 cells - the significant reduction in pathogen adhesion and cytotoxicity was specific to epithelial cells under thermal stress, a detail kept in effect_size rather than smoothed over.*
  - A separately reported 75.7% adhesion reduction and 41.7% invasion inhibition of E. coli used primary chicken intestinal epithelial cells rather than a human model - directionally consistent but not pooled into effect_size above since it is a different (avian) system.
  - Sources: DOI:10.1186/1757-4749-1-14

#### transiently or persistently colonizes the gut

`mech-colonization`

- **humans** · benefit · tier **D** — healthy adults, single-arm colonization studies (no placebo comparator)
  - recovered from colonic biopsies in 4 of 5 subjects after 12 days of consumption (Alander 1997); detectable in feces and biopsies for over a week after discontinuation (Alander 1999) · 2 studies · 10 participants · consistent · independent replication: no
  - ~6x10^10 CFU/day (Alander 1999; dose not confirmed for the 1997 biopsy study) · 12 days consumption; detectable >1 week after discontinuation · fermented whey drink
  - human_observational · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: unclear
  - *Two small uncontrolled human studies from the same research group (Alander et al. 1997, n=5 colonoscopy patients; Alander et al. 1999, n=5 volunteers), both using a whey drink fermented with confirmed strain GG, identified by colony morphology, lactose fermentation, and PCR. No control arm and no comparator strain in either study, so this is descriptive colonization data, not a controlled effect - tier D per uncontrolled human observational evidence. The finding itself (GG attaches to colonic mucosa and persists transiently rather than permanently colonizing) is consistent across both studies.*
  - Both studies note fecal sampling alone understates colonization - mucosal biopsy was needed to detect GG in some subjects fecal-negative at that timepoint.
  - Sources: PMID:9872808; DOI:10.1046/j.1472-765X.1997.00140.x

### Safety

#### bacteremia or fungemia

`safety-bacteremia`

- **humans** · harm · tier **D** — patients with a compromised gut barrier or indwelling central line: immunocompromised/immunosuppressed patients (including active IBD on corticosteroids), short-bowel-syndrome infants, preterm/NICU infants, and critically ill adults with central venous catheters
  - not applicable - case reports have no denominator; genomic/molecular strain typing (WGS, PFGE, or 16S rRNA sequencing) confirmed the bloodstream isolate matched the ingested probiotic strain GG in every case cited here · 5 studies · consistent · independent replication: yes
  - capsule/tablet across the cited cases
  - human_case_report · bias: not_assessed · industry funding: none
  - *Human safety signals of this kind live almost entirely in case reports per source policy, so tier D applies by definition regardless of how many cases exist or how tightly strain identity is confirmed. Five independent, molecularly strain-confirmed cases across four independent publications plus a strain-specific SR's safety note: (1) an immunocompetent trauma patient with a central venous catheter and GI compromise, WGS-confirmed, cleared without antibiotics; (2) an infant with short bowel syndrome, PFGE-confirmed; (3) an adult with severe active ulcerative colitis on corticosteroids, 16S-confirmed, with concurrent candidemia; (4) a pediatric ulcerative-colitis patient, 16S-confirmed, recovered after 5 days of antibiotics; (5) rare sepsis noted in preterm infants in the strain-specific NEC-prevention SR (see gi.json's gi-nec-prevention). Every identified case involves a host-side barrier or line vulnerability, not bacteremia in an otherwise healthy oral consumer - that pattern, not the raw case count, is what independently_replicated:yes and consistency:consistent are tracking here.*
  - Related but not pooled into this row: PROSPECT (a large ICU RCT, see imm-vap in immune.json) found L. rhamnosus recovered from a normally sterile site in 15/1318 (1.1%) probiotic-group ICU patients vs 1/1332 (0.1%) controls - a sterile-site-culture signal in critically ill adults, not a bacteremia case series, but consistent with the same compromised-barrier risk pattern. A single-center NICU cohort also found higher necrotizing enterocolitis incidence after routine LGG adoption (see gi-nec-prevention) - NEC itself is a different outcome from bacteremia, but the two safety signals co-occur in the same preterm population and both argue for caution in NICU use specifically. Several additional L. rhamnosus bacteremia case reports exist in the literature (renal transplant, burn patient, recurrent bacteremia/endocarditis, splenic hematoma) but were excluded here because the reports available did not confirm strain GG specifically versus another L. rhamnosus strain or an unidentified commercial product - per the taxonomy trap discipline, species-level 'L. rhamnosus' case reports are not treated as GG-specific evidence.
  - Sources: DOI:10.1016/j.infpip.2022.100200; PMID:15750472; DOI:10.1007/s15010-015-0798-2; PMID:23426446; PMID:29402455

**Gaps noted:**

- GI: No strain-attributable evidence found for gi-cdiff-prevention specifically: existing trials combine LGG with other strains (e.g. L. reuteri, PLACIDE's mixed lactobacilli/bifidobacteria), and tier rule 6 blocks decomposing those pools. gi-hpylori-adjunct: no dedicated eradication-adjunct trial data beyond the AAD subgroup noted above.
- Immune: imm-vaccine-response: no single-strain LGG RCT with a vaccine-antibody-titer outcome was found - the infant pneumococcal/pertussis trial used a Bifidobacterium+LGG combination product (not decomposable per tier rule 2), and an elderly-influenza and a healthy-adult B-cell trial exist but were not gradeable as a clinical antibody-response claim from the abstracts available. imm-influenza-like was not searched separately from the URTI/respiratory literature above. imm-otitis-media-incidence and imm-cma-oral-tolerance were reconciled into the vocabulary from this pass's proposals; the graded leads are Liu et al. 2013 SR (PMID:23665598, acute otitis media, RR 0.76 [0.64-0.91], NNT 17) and Tan et al. 2021 SR (PMID:34746056, cow's-milk-allergy oral tolerance, RR 2.22 [1.86-2.66]) - neither row is written yet, so both ids sit in this pass's derived debt.
- Metabolic: met-ldl / met-total-cholesterol / met-triglycerides: a synbiotic trial (LGG + soluble corn fiber, Costabile et al. 2017, Saimes study) found reduced LDL and total cholesterol in hypercholesterolemic elderly, but the corn fiber co-intervention is itself cholesterol-lowering and the effect cannot be attributed to LGG alone under tier rule 2's logic; not built as a claim. A second trial (PMC11083618, n=50 Thai elderly, LDL -35.32 mg/dL p=0.045) used an L. rhamnosus product from a Korean manufacturer with no ATCC 53103/GG designation given - strain identity unconfirmed, so also not built as a claim, per the taxonomy trap noted in triage. met-body-weight: an LGG-specific RCT was not found; several body-weight trials surfaced are other Lactobacillus species. met-insulin-resistance: one finding (HOMA-IR improved in pregnancy) used an LGG+BB12 combination product, not decomposable to LGG alone per tier rule 2. met-gestational-diabetes: the well-known positive trial in this space is HN001, a distinct L. rhamnosus strain - not GG, not used here.
- Mental health: mh-depression: no gradeable single-strain GG human trial found - the one positive BDI result used a GG+Bifidobacterium lactis combination product, not decomposable to GG alone per tier rule 2, and a registered pilot (NCT05564767) has no posted results yet. mh-stress-cortisol: the well-known positive academic-stress trial uses strain CNCM I-3690, not GG - excluded per the taxonomy trap noted in triage. mh-autism-symptoms: a pilot RCT of LGG in ADHD (not autism) turned up but ADHD has no matching outcome id and the pilot was not assessed in depth this pass - worth a closer look in a future pass, potentially as a proposed_impacts candidate.
- Mechanistic: mech-vagal-signaling and mech-gaba: the well-known vagotomy-dependent GABA-receptor study (Bravo et al. 2011, PNAS) used strain JB-1, not GG - excluded per the taxonomy trap flagged at triage; no GG-specific vagal or GABA-receptor mechanistic study was found in this pass. mech-hpa-axis: a GG-specific early-life-stress rat study (PMID:29173065) reports 'changes to hippocampal mRNA expression of genes related to stress circuitry' but the abstract does not name specific HPA-axis markers (corticosterone/CRF/glucocorticoid-receptor) with quantitative values, and the significant behavioral effect was for a PDX/GOS-prebiotic-plus-LGG combination rather than an LGG-alone arm - not gradeable as either a clean HPA-axis or decomposable single-entity finding; a closer read of the full text in a future pass may resolve this. mech-bile-salt-hydrolase: literature describes GG as enriching BSH-active taxa in the wider community rather than confirming high intrinsic BSH activity in GG itself; not gradeable as a GG mechanism from what this pass found.
- Safety: No formal incidence rate (cases per million doses sold) was found to contextualize how rare bacteremia/fungemia events are against GG's very high global consumption volume - the case-report literature by its nature cannot supply a denominator, which is exactly why it caps at tier D rather than being read as a meaningful population-level risk estimate.
